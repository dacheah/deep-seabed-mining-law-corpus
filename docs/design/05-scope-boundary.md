# 05 — Scope boundary (Deep Seabed Mining Law Corpus)

Grow **instrument by instrument**, not by sweep. Breadth is worthless if any record's provenance is
doubtful.

## In scope
The law governing mineral resources of **"the Area"** (the seabed and subsoil beyond national
jurisdiction) and the parallel US regime:

- **UNCLOS Part XI** (the Area) and the **1994 Agreement** implementing Part XI.
- **ISA instruments**: the three exploration regulations (polymetallic nodules; polymetallic
  sulphides; cobalt-rich ferromanganese crusts); the draft **Exploitation Regulations** ("the Mining
  Code"); associated **Legal and Technical Commission recommendations**; and ISA **decisions,
  standards and guidelines** (`ISBA/...`).
- **ITLOS decisions concerning the Area** — advisory opinions, judgments and orders of the Tribunal
  and of its Seabed Disputes Chamber. Includes the **2011 Seabed Disputes Chamber Advisory Opinion**
  (Case 17), the key case law on sponsoring-state responsibility and liability.
  *(Extended 2026-07-18 from "Case 17 only" — see JC-007. **Party pleadings** — applications,
  memorials, written submissions — are **not** in scope: they are authentic official filings but they
  are not law. They are recorded in `queue/candidates.md` so the trail exists. Extended 2026-09-17:
  **hearing transcripts** (verbatim records) and **compliance filings** (party reports to the Tribunal,
  including the ISA's initial report under the provisional measures) are likewise **not** in scope. A
  record of the proceedings' own state under supervision would be a new layer with its own rules, not
  an extension of this one — see JC-007's 2026-09-17 status.)*
- **Sponsoring-state national laws** enabling contractors under the ISA regime. Held for Nauru and
  Tonga from issue #13 (2026-09-18): Nauru's International Seabed Minerals Act 2015, SBMA Act 2024 and
  SBMA Regulations 2025, and Tonga's Seabed Minerals Act 2014 in the Attorney General's Office 2020
  Revised Edition (CAP 20.07). The ISA's National Legislation Database is a finding aid - it reproduces
  State submissions "in the form and the language(s) received" and does not edit or translate them - so
  the artefact of record is the State's own file, each held with a hash-verified source URL. Reuse terms
  are unstated on every one of them (JC-013). One version per instrument: for Tonga the AGO's current
  revised edition, not the 2016 edition or the as-enacted 2014 text it also publishes.
- The **US non-UNCLOS parallel track**: DSHMRA and its NOAA regulations (15 CFR 970/971), plus
  Outer Continental Shelf activity under OCSLA/BOEM — captured but clearly marked as the
  non-UNCLOS alternative (JC-001).

## Out of scope (until a dated decision)
- General law of the sea unrelated to the Area (navigation, EEZ resources, continental-shelf claims
  under Part VI, delimitation).
- Fisheries law (incl. the Central Arctic Ocean fisheries agreement — a *comparative reference* for the
  concept layer only, not authoritative-layer law).
- General environmental law and the Antarctic mining moratorium (Madrid Protocol) — comparative
  reference only.
- ISA **DeepData** scientific/environmental datasets — an adjacency, explicitly **not law**; never
  ingested into the authoritative layer.

## Borderline items
Anything on the law-of-the-sea / fisheries / environmental-law edge goes to `queue/candidates.md` for
a **recorded decision**, not straight into the corpus. Record every scope change as a dated note.
